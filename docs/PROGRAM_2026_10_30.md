## 2. The learned cascade as a latent-variable model

**The idea in one line.** One model family does both jobs: it chooses a smaller pool, and it ranks the pool it chose; the
two are trained against each other, and what is learned is distilled into a fixed rule.

**The latent is a depth.** Take the rule's pool in some order (the first stage's rrf, or a cheap pass-1 score). Let `k`
be the depth at which it is cut, from a short grid (50, 100, 200, 300, 500, 1,000, 2,000, all). A question has one latent
`k`, so the model is a mixture over about eight components, not a search over subsets: the sum is exact and nothing needs
a sampled gradient.

| piece | what it is | where it is trained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `p_psi(k \| q)` | the selector: a distribution over depths from the question and the pool's cheap measures; no gold | in the deployed path |
| `p_theta(G \| top-k, q)` | the ranker's likelihood of the question's golds among the rows kept: a softmax over the kept rows for each gold, with a floor `eps` for a gold that is not kept | the ranker, as today |
| the cost | `lambda * rows(k)`, subtracted from the bound below | a constant of the stage |

**The bound.** For each question, with `log p(G | q) = log sum_k p_psi(k | q) p_theta(G | top-k, q)`,

    log p(G | q)  >=  E_{r(k)} [ log p_theta(G | top-k, q) ]  -  KL( r(k) || p_psi(k | q) )  -  lambda * E_{r(k)} [ rows(k) ]

with `r(k)`, the responsibility, free to depend on the gold (Jensen's inequality). The best `r` is
`r(k) ∝ p_psi(k | q) * p_theta(G | top-k, q) * exp(-lambda * rows(k))`. This is the E-step and it is exact. Two things
follow, and they are why the form is worth a stage:
1. **Shrinking the pool raises the likelihood only while every gold stays in.** A smaller pool makes the softmax
   sharper, so the likelihood of an in-pool gold rises; a gold outside costs `eps`. So `r` concentrates on the shallowest
   depth that holds the golds. That is the oracle depth, the 111-row figure. The ranker and the pool are pulled toward each
   other by one objective, which is the joint training the user described.
2. **The selector cannot see the gold.** `p_psi(k | q)` is trained to match `r` (the M-step for `psi` is the cross-entropy
   to the responsibilities). The gap between `r` (gold known) and `p_psi` (gold unknown) is the amortization gap, and it
   is exactly the thing stage 0 measured in another form: in-domain the selector can predict the depth, zero-shot it
   could not. The ELBO is tight only as far as the selector generalizes.

The M-step for `theta` weights each question's ranker loss over depths by `r(k)`, so the ranker is trained on pools of the
sizes the selector will hand it.

**The dial is lambda.** `lambda` charges for rows. One value for all six datasets is "the level" of the open ruling in
another form: a large `lambda` is the 2,000-row construction, a small one is the 3,300-row construction. Choosing it is
the user's ruling (section 7), and the stage reports the curve, not a point.

**The cut at deployment is a quantile of the predicted depth.** The selector outputs a distribution over depths; the
cut is its tau-quantile (tau = 0.95 or 0.96 is the level). This is the "distribution" form of the idea and needs no ELBO:
a calibrated quantile of the last gold's depth. Calibration is by leave-one-dataset-out, so the coverage shortfall on an
unseen graph is measured, not assumed.

**What the ELBO is and is not.** It is a way to write the loss so that the selector, the ranker and the cost have one
objective and an exact E-step. It is not a promise that the model gains: the amortization gap can eat all of the gain.
The first stage below tests the gap itself, with no model.

## 3. The shared latent space

Two options, run in this order.
1. **Separate heads, separate inputs (the first form).** The selector and the ranker are two models. The selector reads
   per-question scalars that exist before any row is scored (first-stage measures, walk-mass measures, the stored
   `mq` columns); the ranker is today's. This keeps the MLP's rule (inputs only from before any row is scored) and makes
   attribution clean: a gain from the cut is the cut's.
2. **One encoder, two heads (the shared latent).** A per-row hidden state `h_v(q)` feeds the ranker's score head and a
   selector head; the per-question depth distribution reads a pooled statistic of the `h_v`. For the GNN this is its own
   hidden state after message passing, and the pooling is the GNN's. For the MLP, a pooled statistic of scored rows is a
   pool-level quantity computed after scoring. The user ruled on 31 October that this is fine (section 9, item 2), so the
   MLP's selector may read it; form 1 stays the first run for clean attribution.

Form 2 is run only if form 1's first EM round gains, as an ablation: same data, same rounds, shared against separate.

## 4. The failure modes of EM here, and what each one gets

| failure | why it is expected here | what stops it | the check that would show it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The selector learns to drop hard golds and the loop reinforces it | `r` is built from the ranker's current likelihood; a gold the ranker finds hard gets low `r` at shallow depths and the selector learns "deep for this kind", but a gold it never keeps never returns | the support is the rule's pool, so reach cannot fall below the pool's; `eps` is small but nonzero and the posterior is mixed with a uniform share over deeper depths; round 0 is the baseline; training pools always contain the golds (forced in) | ALL per dataset after every round against round 0; stop at the first drop of more than 0.005 on any dataset |
| In-domain gain, zero-shot loss (em-gr; stage 0) | the selector fits the training graphs' depth statistics | leave-one-dataset-out inside every round (fit on the other datasets, accept on the held one); group-DRO weights so the worst dataset sets the update; selector inputs are per-question quantiles, not raw scores | acceptance is only the held-out read; an in-domain number is never a pass |
| The ranker over-fits the shrunken pools | after the cut the easy negatives are gone, so at deployment a selector error hands the ranker rows it never saw | the ranker's training pools are a mixture over depths with a fixed share at full depth; the end-to-end R@5 (selector then ranker) is the only graded number | end-to-end R@5 against the ranker alone on the full pool |
| Oscillation | each step's target is the other's previous output | damped updates (an exponential average of `psi`, one step of `psi` per ranker epoch); two or three rounds, not a convergence run; each round's pools are an immutable artifact | the per-round table of rows, ALL and R@5; a round that moves ALL by more than the floor and R@5 by less is called out |
| Collapse to a fixed K | the selector ignores the question and returns one depth, so the KL term is zero | none is needed: that outcome is the fixed rule | the selector is compared with the best fixed K and the best two-level router (9114a11) at the same rows; it must beat both |
| The tail is the cost | musique's 12% needing 1,000 or more nodes sets the shared cost | the selector is trained on a quantile (pinball) loss at tau, not on the mean, so the tail is what it is fitted to | rows and ALL by dataset, with musique and webqsp read first |
| Gold leakage | `r` uses the gold | the gold enters the training targets only; the deployed path has no gold; carves are s1sel for fits and choices, s1eval for reads, test splits never | the existing leak guards on the carves |
| Normalising over the pool inside an MLP input | em-gr did it | the MLP's selector reads only pre-scoring scalars (section 3 form 1) | the input list is printed with the declaration |

## 5. How the learnings reach a fixed rule (the generalization path)

The learned selector is an upper bound and a probe; the paper's rule is the fixed one. After each round:
1. **Distil.** Fit the simplest rule that reproduces the selector's depths from the pre-scoring measures: a single-signal
   two-level router (the dense top score is the first candidate), a monotone piecewise-linear depth in two or three
   measures, or a depth-quantile rule with one global parameter. The fit uses the selector's outputs, never the golds.
2. **Grade the rule as U2s grades pools:** rows and ALL by dataset, the level, the weights, leave-one-dataset-out.
3. **Accept** the distilled rule only if it is within one declared margin of the selector at the same rows, and it replaces
   a construction only by the screen rules. The selector's own number is reported beside, as the gap between learned and
   fixed.
4. **Generalization checks that are in every stage:** each dataset held out in turn; webqsp's s1eval read beside; the
   passage graphs and the KB graphs reported separately; pooled quantile thresholds instead of absolute ones, so a
   threshold set on four datasets means something on the fifth.

If the selector does not beat the two-level router at equal rows, the loop has taught the rule nothing, and the answer to
the level ruling stays a construction choice, not a model.

## 6. Iterative improvement beyond a smaller pool

A cut shrinks what the ranker scores. It does not raise reach, and it does not shorten the walk, so cold latency is
unchanged for the build (walks dominate it, P4a); the forward pass shrinks, which helps the GNN more than the MLP. The one
form that can raise reach is to let the ranker's top rows seed a second walk. It costs a second walk on the cold path, and
the evidence-flow forms were closed with no signal on passage graphs (universal-v2). It is **not declared**: it would be
written only after the first EM round shows a reach-limited dataset (musique, metaqa, webqsp) moving, and only after
checking it against that closure.
